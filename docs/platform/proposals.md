# Proposals

Devplan creates proposals from connected customer feedback and product context. It brings a suggested scope, customer demand, and supporting evidence together so your team can decide which opportunities deserve a closer look.

Use Proposals when deciding what to build next. Instead of assembling a case from scattered conversations, start with the need Devplan has identified, inspect the sources, and develop the strongest ideas into projects.

## Evaluate a proposal

Open **Proposals** and select an item. A proposal can include:

- **Demand summary:** the problem or opportunity behind the suggestion.
- **Customers referenced:** the customers connected to that demand, with account information where available.
- **Proposed scope and relevant product areas:** what the work could address and how it relates to your product.
- **Evidence:** the conversations, documents, signals, or issues supporting the proposal. Open these references to understand the original request and its context.

For example, when reviewing a proposal to improve onboarding, look at which customers encountered the problem, what prevented them from progressing, and whether the suggested scope addresses that need. This gives your team a concrete basis for discussion before committing to a project.

### Compare priority

Where available, Devplan provides an estimated **RICE score**, combining reach, impact, confidence, and effort. You can sort proposals by this score and hover over a score to see its explanation.

Use the estimate to decide what to examine first, then weigh the evidence against your priorities and existing work. Missing customer information or a missing score means Devplan lacks that context; it does not establish that the opportunity has no value.

## Develop it into a project

When a proposal is worth pursuing, select **Define project**. Weaver opens a conversation with the proposal's demand summary, scope, product context, and evidence notes already available. Work with Weaver to create a brief, adding constraints or clarifying the outcome you want.

Once the brief is generated, Devplan moves the proposal into **Projects** and opens its definition. Continue refining requirements and planning the work there. The proposal provides the starting case for the work; your team decides its priority and delivery commitments.

## Keep the collection useful

Search for relevant proposals, sort by recency or RICE score, and add tags to organize them. **Archive** suggestions you do not want in the active collection.

If the collection is empty or lacks useful evidence, check your [integrations](/integrations-overview). Customer conversations, meeting notes, support tickets, and CRM records give Devplan more context for identifying worthwhile work.
